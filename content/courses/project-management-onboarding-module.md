Draft of 2026-10-30. The process stages, timelines and tool names are proposed defaults. Edit them to match how the team actually works.

This module takes a new project manager from day one to running client projects across all ten services we sell. Work through it in your first two weeks, then use it as a reference.

By the end you should be able to explain what we sell, run a kickoff, build a realistic timeline, and report status to a client without help.

## 2. Your role as project manager

You are the single point of contact between the client and the specialists. Specialists do the work; you make sure the right work is done, on time, and that the client always knows where things stand.

**You are responsible for**

- Turning the sale into a clear scope, deliverables list and timeline
- Collecting every input from the client (access, brand assets, approvals) before work starts
- Assigning tasks to developers, designers, SEO specialists, marketers and writers
- Tracking progress daily and flagging risk early
- Sending client updates on a fixed rhythm
- Controlling scope: nothing extra is built without a written change
- Closing the project with a handover and a review

**You are not responsible for** doing the specialist work yourself, or for promising results we do not control (rankings, ROAS, sales).

**Working rules**

1. Never promise a date you have not checked with the person doing the work.
2. Bad news goes to the client early, with a plan, never at the deadline.
3. Every client decision is confirmed in writing, in the project channel or by email.
4. If a task is blocked for more than one working day, escalate it.
5. Answer client messages within one working day, even if the answer is "checking, update by tomorrow".

## 4. The standard project process

Every project, whatever the service, moves through the same six stages. The work inside each stage changes by service (see the playbooks), but the gates do not.

1. **Handover from sales.** Read the proposal, confirm scope, price, and promised dates with the salesperson. Exit gate: you can state the scope in three sentences.
2. **Kickoff and onboarding.** Hold a kickoff call, agree goals and success measures, name the client contact, and request all access and assets. Exit gate: written scope, timeline and access checklist approved by the client.
3. **Planning.** Break scope into tasks, assign owners and dates, set up the project board and the client channel. Exit gate: every task has an owner and a due date.
4. **Execution.** Specialists deliver in short cycles. You track daily, remove blockers, and send the weekly update. Exit gate: each deliverable passes internal review before the client sees it.
5. **Review and approval.** The client reviews, you collect consolidated feedback, and the team revises. Limit revision rounds to what the proposal allows (default: two). Exit gate: written client approval.
6. **Launch, handover and review.** Go live or publish, hand over documents and logins, report results, and run an internal retro. For retainers this becomes the monthly report and plan.

**Kickoff checklist**

- [ ] Goals, target customer and success measures written down
- [ ] Scope and exclusions read back to the client
- [ ] Access received (store, hosting, analytics, Search Console, ad accounts, social accounts as relevant)
- [ ] Brand assets, product data and existing content received
- [ ] Approver named, and review turnaround agreed (default: two working days)
- [ ] Communication rhythm agreed (channel, weekly update day)

**Internal review checklist before anything goes to a client**

- [ ] Matches the written scope
- [ ] Checked by someone other than the person who made it
- [ ] No broken links, typos or placeholder content
- [ ] Tested on mobile and desktop where relevant

## 5. Timelines

These timelines come from how the team actually delivers. The clock starts only when all client inputs and access are received. Client review and approval time is not included in the figures below, so add it as separate tasks and expect dates to move when reviews or approvals are late.

| Service | Timeline | What to remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shopify website development | 4 weeks, can go higher | Starts after all inputs are received. Excludes client review time. Reviews and approvals extend it. |
| Shopify UI/UX | 2 weeks | Covers the key pages. Subject to reviews and approvals. A separate service from development, so it finishes before development starts. |
| Technical SEO | Detailed audit in 5 business days, then 2 to 3 days after the audit | The 2 to 3 days is the post-audit step. Larger fix lists are scoped separately. |
| E-commerce SEO | Keyword research in 5 business days, then about 50 products a month | Scales with catalogue size (see 5.1). |
| On-page SEO | Same as e-commerce SEO | Varies with the number of pages. |
| Off-page SEO | Setup 2 to 3 weeks, then a monthly process | On-page work is done during the setup period. |
| Social media management | Monthly process | The first month includes setup: brand voice and the first content calendar. |
| Performance marketing | Setup 1 to 2 weeks after keyword research | Best launched once on-page work is done. Allow a minimum of 2 to 4 weeks to judge results. Tracking must be verified before any spend. |
| Dropshipper research and product identification | 1 to 2 weeks | |
| Content marketing and blogs | First batch in 3 to 4 weeks, then monthly or weekly depending on requirement | Batch size is whatever the client has bought. Confirm the article count at kickoff. |

### 5.1 E-commerce and on-page SEO scale with catalogue size

At about 50 products a month, the time to cover a whole catalogue grows quickly. Tell the client this at the start and begin with bestsellers and top collections so they see results early.

| Products in catalogue | Approximate time to cover all products |
| --- | --- |
| 50 | 1 month |
| 150 | 3 months |
| 300 | 6 months |
| 500 | 10 months |

For on-page SEO on non-product pages, quote by the number of pages in the agreed list.

### 5.2 Order of work for a client buying several services

Each service is delivered on its own timeline, and the order below is the usual sequence. UI/UX and development do not overlap: the designs are approved before the build starts.

1. Shopify UI/UX (2 weeks)
2. Shopify website development (4 weeks or more)
3. Technical SEO audit (5 business days), ideally before launch
4. Keyword research (5 business days)
5. On-page and e-commerce SEO (about 50 products a month), with off-page setup running alongside (2 to 3 weeks)
6. Performance marketing setup (1 to 2 weeks after keyword research), launching once priority pages are done
7. Social media and content run in parallel as monthly processes

**What "on-page done" means here:** the priority landing pages and collections are optimised. It does not mean the whole catalogue is finished, because on-page work continues month after month.

### 5.3 Rules that protect the timeline

- Do not start performance marketing spend until conversion tracking is verified.
- Do not judge ad results before 2 to 4 weeks of data.
- Do not promise ranking changes from off-page work in the first weeks. Links take time to earn and to count.
- Confirm the number of articles, posts or products in scope at kickoff, because it sets the monthly workload.

**How to build a timeline**

1. List every deliverable and the specialist who owns it.
2. Ask each owner for an estimate in working days, not a deadline.
3. Add client waiting time (reviews, content, access) as its own tasks.
4. Add a buffer of about 20 percent for projects and 10 percent for retainer setup.
5. Share the timeline with the client in writing and ask for confirmation.

**When a date will slip**

- Tell the client as soon as you know, with the cause, the new date and what you are doing about it.
- If the cause is a late client input, record it in writing so the revised date is agreed rather than argued.
- Never silently move a date on the board.

## 6. Service playbooks

For each service: what you need from the client, the main steps, what to check before delivery, and what usually goes wrong.

### 6.1 Shopify website development

- **Need from client:** store access, brand assets, product data or spreadsheet, policies, domain and payment details, reference sites
- **Steps:** theme choice or custom theme, structure and navigation, product and collection setup, apps and integrations, payments, shipping and taxes, redirects, staging review, launch
- **QA:** test checkout end to end, mobile and desktop, page speed, redirects, tracking installed
- **Common risks:** product data arrives late, too many apps slow the store, scope grows into custom features

### 6.2 Shopify UI/UX

- **Need from client:** brand guide, target customers, analytics access, competitors they admire
- **Steps:** research and audit, user flows, wireframes, visual designs, prototype, feedback rounds, developer handover
- **QA:** designs match brand, spacing and states are consistent, mobile views included
- **Common risks:** too many stakeholders giving feedback, opinions replacing data

### 6.3 Technical SEO

- **Need from client:** Search Console, analytics, store or CMS access, developer contact
- **Steps:** crawl, indexing and sitemap review, speed and Core Web Vitals, structured data, duplicate and canonical issues, redirect and 404 cleanup, prioritised fix list, implementation, re-crawl
- **QA:** re-crawl shows fixes applied, nothing important blocked, sitemap and robots checked
- **Common risks:** platform limits, a fix list the client never approves, changes that break the theme

### 6.4 E-commerce SEO

- **Need from client:** catalogue export, margins and bestsellers, competitors, Search Console
- **Steps:** keyword research by category and product, mapping keywords to collections and products, collection and product page optimisation, internal linking, monthly review
- **QA:** each target page has one clear keyword focus, no duplicated titles, changes logged
- **Common risks:** thousands of products and no priority, client rewriting optimised copy

### 6.5 On-page SEO

- **Need from client:** list of priority pages, approval to edit, Search Console
- **Steps:** page audit, keyword and intent check, titles, meta descriptions, headings, copy edits, schema where needed, internal links, before and after tracking
- **QA:** changes match the approved sheet, no keyword stuffing, pages still read well
- **Common risks:** no baseline recorded, so no proof of progress

### 6.6 Off-page SEO

- **Need from client:** Search Console and analytics access, list of competitors, brand and product details, approval for outreach and guest content, any past link-building history or penalties
- **Steps:** backlink audit of the client and competitors, link gap analysis, strategy and target list, outreach and guest posts, digital PR and brand mentions, directory and citation listings, disavow of harmful links if needed, monthly link report
- **QA:** every link is from a relevant, real site; anchor text looks natural; no paid-link schemes or private blog networks; all placed links logged with URL and date
- **Common risks:** links take months to earn and the client expects fast ranking changes, low-quality links that risk a Google penalty, slow replies from site owners, no baseline of the existing backlink profile

### 6.7 Social media management

- **Need from client:** brand voice, assets, product list, account access or admin rights, approver
- **Steps:** audience and platform plan, content calendar, creatives and captions, client approval, scheduling and posting, community replies, monthly report
- **QA:** calendar approved before posting, links and tags checked, brand voice consistent
- **Common risks:** late approvals, client posting off-calendar

### 6.8 Performance marketing

- **Need from client:** ad account access, budget, margins, pixel and analytics access, product feed
- **Steps:** goals and target return, tracking check, account structure, creatives and copy, launch, daily checks, weekly optimisation, monthly report
- **QA:** conversion tracking verified before spend, budgets and targeting match the plan
- **Common risks:** broken tracking, low budget against goals, ad account disapprovals, a client expecting results in days

### 6.9 Dropshipper research and product identification

- **Need from client:** niche or budget, target country, platform, any products already tried
- **Steps:** market and trend scan, competitor store review, product shortlist, supplier checks, margin and shipping estimate, risk notes, final recommendations
- **QA:** each product has demand evidence, a landed cost, a margin estimate and a supplier alternative
- **Common risks:** treating a shortlist as a guarantee of sales, unreliable suppliers

### 6.10 Content marketing and blogs

- **Need from client:** audience, products, competitors, tone, any subject-matter input
- **Steps:** topic and keyword plan, briefs, writing, edit, add images and links, client approval, publish, track performance
- **QA:** matches the brief, facts checked, internal links added, plagiarism and readability checked
- **Common risks:** thin or generic content, slow approvals, no internal subject expert

## 8. Client management

Clients stay when they feel informed and in control, even when results take time. Keep a steady rhythm and keep everything in writing.

**Communication rhythm**

| What | When | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kickoff call | Once, at the start | Goals, scope, timeline, access, ways of working |
| Weekly update | Same day every week | Done this week, planned next week, blockers, what we need from the client |
| Review call | At each approval milestone | Walk through work, collect consolidated feedback |
| Monthly report | First week of the month (retainers) | Results, work done, learnings, next month's plan |

**Weekly update template**

```
Subject: [Client] weekly update, [date]

Done this week:
- ...

Planned next week:
- ...

Needed from you (with date):
- ...

Risks or changes:
- ...
```

**Handling scope changes**

1. Say what was requested and how it differs from the agreed scope.
2. Estimate the extra time and cost with the specialist.
3. Send the client a written change with price and the effect on the timeline.
4. Start the work only after written approval.

**Escalation**

- Client unresponsive for five working days: send a reminder, then involve the account owner.
- Client unhappy with quality: acknowledge, agree what is wrong, bring in a senior reviewer, and set a fix date.
- Payment problems, legal threats or abusive behaviour: stop and tell management the same day.
- Specialist blocked or overloaded: tell the team lead the same day, before the date is at risk.

**Setting expectations on results.** SEO and ads need time and testing. Promise activity, quality and reporting. Do not promise rankings, traffic, sales or a return on ad spend.

## 9. Quality, reporting and KPIs

Report on two things: the work we did, and the results it produced. Always record a baseline at the start so progress can be shown.

| Service | Work measures | Result measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shopify website development | Milestones delivered on time, bugs found at QA | Go-live date, page speed, checkout test passed |
| Shopify UI/UX | Screens delivered, feedback rounds used | Client approval, conversion rate and bounce rate after launch |
| Technical SEO | Issues found and fixed | Indexed pages, crawl errors, Core Web Vitals |
| E-commerce SEO | Pages optimised | Organic traffic, impressions, rankings, organic revenue |
| On-page SEO | Pages updated against the plan | Click-through rate and rankings for target pages |
| Off-page SEO | Outreach sent, links placed, citations built | New referring domains, link quality, rankings for target pages, referral traffic |
| Social media management | Posts published against calendar | Reach, engagement, follower growth, traffic from social |
| Performance marketing | Campaigns live, tests run | Spend, conversions, cost per acquisition, return on ad spend |
| Dropshipper research | Products researched and vetted | Shortlist accepted, margin estimates, supplier confirmations |
| Content marketing and blogs | Articles published against plan | Organic traffic to articles, rankings, assisted conversions |

**Project health measures for you**

- On-time delivery of milestones
- Number of revision rounds against the number agreed
- Open blockers older than one working day
- Client response time on approvals
- Scope changes raised in writing

**Monthly report structure:** one-line summary, results against baseline, work completed, what we learned, plan for next month, anything we need from the client.

**Glossary**

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline | Starting numbers recorded before work begins, used to show progress |
| Scope | The exact list of work agreed and what is excluded |
| Change request | A written, priced addition to the agreed scope |
| Retainer | A monthly service that renews and is reported monthly |
| Staging | A private copy of the store used to test changes before they go live |
| Core Web Vitals | Google's page experience measures for loading, interactivity and layout stability |
| Search Console | Google's free tool showing how a site appears and performs in search |
| ROAS | Return on ad spend: revenue from ads divided by ad cost |
| CPA | Cost per acquisition: ad cost divided by conversions |
| Landed cost | Product cost plus shipping and duties to reach the customer |
| Internal linking | Links between pages of the same site that help users and search engines |
| Backlink | A link from another website to the client's site |
| Referring domain | A unique website that links to the client's site (more useful to count than total links) |
| Anchor text | The clickable words of a link |
| Handover | The formal end of a project: files, logins, documents and a summary passed to the client |
