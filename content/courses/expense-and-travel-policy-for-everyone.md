This course is for everyone in the organisation: developers, designers, marketers, writers, project managers, finance, admin, sales, leaders, interns and freelancers. It explains how to spend company money wisely, how to get approval, how to book travel, how to buy tools and software, how to keep receipts, how to claim and get reimbursed, how to handle client money, and what counts as a false claim. ## 1. Why everyone needs an expense and travel policy

Almost everyone spends company money at some point: a taxi to a client meeting, a stock photo licence, a team lunch, a flight to an event, a plugin for a Shopify store. A clear policy protects three groups. It protects you, because you know what will be paid back. It protects the organisation, because money is spent on purpose and with care. It protects our clients, because their money, including ad budgets and platform charges, is never mixed up with ours.

**This course will help you**

- Know what you can and cannot claim, before you spend
- Get the right approval at the right time
- Book travel and stays sensibly and safely
- Keep the receipts and invoices that finance needs
- Submit claims that are quick to approve and quick to pay
- Handle client money, vendors and freelancer invoices properly
- Spot and avoid false or careless claims

**The rules to remember**

1. **Approve first, spend second.** For anything unusual or large, get written approval before you pay.
2. **Business purpose and reasonable cost.** Every expense must be for the organisation's work, at a fair price.
3. **Honest and documented.** Claim only what you really spent, with proof, and never mix personal and business costs.

**How to use this course.** Read chapters 1 and 2 first, as they apply to everyone. Use chapters 3 and 4 to check what you can spend and who approves it. Read chapters 5 to 9 before you travel, entertain a client or buy software. Chapters 10 to 12 explain receipts, claims and the client money rules, which every project manager and marketer must know. Chapters 13 to 15 explain fraud, audits, common mistakes and a worked example. Use chapter 16 for common questions and chapter 17 for the glossary and a short knowledge check. If you are unsure, ask [finance@company] before you spend.

## 2. The principles: how we spend company money

Rules cannot cover every situation. When they do not, these four principles decide.

### Spend it as if it were your own

Ask yourself: would I pay this much for this if it were my money? If the answer is no, choose a cheaper option or ask first. This does not mean being stingy. It means being sensible and fair.

### There must be a business purpose

An expense must help the organisation do its work, such as meeting a client, running a campaign, learning a skill needed for your role, or keeping the team running. If it is mainly for you, your family or friends, it is personal. Do not claim it.

### The cost must be reasonable

Reasonable means a fair price for what is needed, in that place and at that time. A cab for a late meeting after public transport stops is reasonable. A premium car for a short daytime trip may not be, unless there is a good reason.

### Be honest and transparent

- Claim only what you actually spent
- Describe it truthfully, with the date, place, purpose and people involved
- Do not split a bill to avoid approval limits
- Do not ask someone else to claim for you, or claim for something someone else paid for
- If you make a mistake, tell finance. A correction is fine, a cover-up is not

### Which principle applies?

| Situation | Principle in play | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You want a 5-star hotel because you like it | Reasonable cost | Choose a hotel within your grade limit, or ask for approval with a reason |
| Your client dinner included a family member | Business purpose | Claim only the business portion, or pay yourself. Disclose it |
| You lost a receipt for a cab | Honesty | Tell finance, give the details and use the missing receipt form. Do not make one up |
| A vendor says "I'll give you a discount if you pay in cash" | Honesty, transparency | Ask for a proper invoice. Talk to finance if unsure |

## 13. Expense fraud, false claims and speaking up

Most people claim honestly. Mistakes happen and are fixed. Fraud is deliberate dishonesty, and it is serious.

### What counts as a false claim

| Type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Inflated claim | Claiming [Rs 1,800] for a cab that cost [Rs 900] |
| Duplicate claim | Claiming the same bill twice, or claiming something the client or the company already paid |
| Personal items | Including a family dinner or shopping in a business claim |
| Fake or altered receipts | Creating, editing, buying or backdating bills |
| Padding | Claiming a per diem for days you were not travelling |
| Splitting | Dividing a purchase to avoid approval limits |
| Misusing a company card | Using it for personal spending |
| Collusion | Agreeing with a vendor to inflate an invoice |

### Honest mistakes versus fraud

| Honest mistake | Fraud |
| --- | --- |
| A missing date on a receipt | An edited date |
| A personal item included by accident, and reported when found | A personal item hidden in a business bill |
| A late claim | A claim for something that did not happen |

**If you make a mistake,** tell finance before they find it. Correct it and carry on. Honest, early correction is respected.

### Consequences

Depending on the facts, consequences can include rejection of the claim, repayment, a warning, formal disciplinary action under the process in HR Policies Handbook for Everyone and Code of Conduct and Ethics for Everyone, ending the engagement, and reporting to authorities where the law requires. The organisation may recover amounts wrongly claimed, as allowed by law. [confirm with HR or a legal adviser]

### How to report a concern

If you see or suspect a false claim, or you are pressured to approve or submit one:

1. Do not confront the person alone
2. Note what you saw, with dates and amounts, without searching for evidence in places you should not access
3. Report to [finance@company], [HR], or the confidential channel in Speaking Up (Whistleblowing and Grievance Handling)
4. You may report anonymously where [the channel] allows it
5. Raising a concern in good faith is protected. Retaliation is not allowed

## 16. Employee questions and sample answers

These answers are general and use the proposed defaults. Check the written policy for exact limits.

**1. Do I need approval for every small expense?**
Not for everyday items within the limit, such as a local cab or a courier. You need it first for travel by air, overnight stays, anything above [Rs 5,000], client entertainment, software and anything unusual.

**2. Can I claim a cab to the office from home?**
No. Your normal commute is personal. A cab after late client work, when public transport is not safe or available, may be claimed with a reason and your manager's approval.

**3. What if I lose a receipt?**
Tell finance and use the missing receipt declaration, with your manager's approval. Claims without receipts are limited to [Rs 500 per claim and Rs 2,000 per month]. Please never recreate a receipt.

**4. Why does finance keep asking for the company's GSTIN on invoices?**
So the company can claim tax credit on eligible costs, which reduces the true cost. Ask for a GST invoice in the company's name at the time of payment. Finance can tell you what is eligible. [confirm with HR or a legal adviser]

**5. Can I extend a business trip over the weekend?**
Yes, if your manager agrees, but the extra days are personal. Pay for them yourself, and make sure only the business part is in the claim.

**6. The client paid for my lunch. Can I still claim a per diem?**
Claim a reduced per diem, as in the policy. The default reduces it by [Rs 300] per meal provided. Honesty about this keeps claims quick to approve.

**7. Can I use my personal card to pay for a client's ad spend?**
No. Client ad budgets and platform charges are client money. Use the client's payment method or the company account set up for that, with written approval and a log of each spend.

**8. A client's ad account ran out of funds on a sale day. What now?**
Ask the client for an urgent top-up and tell your manager. If a pause will cause real loss, your manager can arrange approved options. Do not put the charge on your own card.

**9. Can I buy a software subscription myself and claim it?**
Not unless it was approved through the route in chapter 8. Unapproved tools can put client data at risk. Raise a request first. See Acceptable Use of IT and AI Tools for Everyone.

**10. How soon should I submit a claim?**
Within [15 days] of the expense or of returning from a trip. Claims older than [60 days] may be rejected. Submit before [the 25th] to be paid in the same month.

**11. How long does reimbursement take?**
After approval and the finance check, normally by [the 10th of the following month], or within [7 working days] of approval. If it takes longer, write to [finance@company] with the claim number.

**12. Can I split a bill to stay under a limit?**
No. Splitting is treated as avoiding approval, even if the total is small. If the total is above your limit, ask for approval.

**13. Is it okay to take a gift from a vendor?**
Only small, modest items within the gift policy, disclosed as required. Anything larger, or anything offered during a tender or negotiation, should be declined and reported. Follow [the gifts and hospitality policy].

**14. Can I give a gift to a government officer to speed up a licence?**
No. Never offer or pay anything of value to a public official without written approval from [the compliance lead]. Follow [the gifts and hospitality policy]. [confirm with HR or a legal adviser]

**15. How do I pay a freelancer?**
Through finance, against an approved agreement and invoice. Do not pay from your own money or in cash. Finance checks the details, deducts tax where required and pays within [30 days].

**16. A vendor says their bank details have changed. What do I do?**
Do not pay on the basis of an email or a message. Call the vendor on a number you already know and tell finance. Fake bank-change requests are a common scam.

**17. My manager asked me to claim a personal expense as a business one. What should I do?**
Do not do it, and do not argue alone. Raise it with finance, HR or the Speaking Up channel. You are protected from retaliation for raising a concern in good faith.

**18. I made a mistake in a claim that was already paid. What now?**
Tell finance as soon as you notice. They will correct it, usually by adjusting the next payment. Honest, early correction is not treated as fraud.

**19. What happens if I do not settle my advance?**
Finance will remind you, and you cannot get a new advance until it is settled. Unsettled amounts can be recovered as agreed and as the law allows. [confirm with HR or a legal adviser]

**20. Do mileage claims need fuel receipts?**
No. Mileage rates already cover fuel. Keep a log of date, route and purpose, and claim parking and tolls separately with receipts.

**21. Can interns and freelancers claim expenses?**
Yes, if approved in advance and covered by their agreement. Interns follow the same rules as employees for approval and receipts. Freelancers usually add approved costs to their invoice.

**22. What if I am asked to explain an old claim during a spot check?**
Answer calmly and provide the original receipt or scan. Spot checks are routine. If you realise there is a mistake, say so early.

**23. Who approves my manager's expenses?**
Their own manager, and finance for larger amounts. Nobody approves their own expenses or those of someone who reports to them.

**24. Can I buy a laptop accessory myself if my laptop is slow and I'm on deadline?**
Raise an urgent request with your manager and [IT head]. In genuine emergencies they can approve quick purchases through the approved route, or ask you to buy with written approval afterward.

**25. I am travelling alone and do not feel safe in the cheapest hotel. What should I do?**
Choose a safer option and tell your manager. Safety comes before savings. Add a short note to your claim. See Health, Safety and Wellbeing for Everyone.
